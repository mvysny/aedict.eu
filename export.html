--- title: "Export" ---

Custom export format

Aedict exports entries as a CSV file for desktop Anki, a spreadsheet or any other program, in a format you write yourself: Aedict Settings / Export / Custom CSV Export Format. Aedict Online has the same setting in its Settings, under Export. Each entry is exported to a single line, the format with every variable replaced by the entry's value. For example:

"%kanji%"\t"%kanji% [%reading%]"\t%sense_no_flags%\t%examples%

produces the following output:

"母"<tab>"母 [はは, はわ, かか, おも, いろは, あも]"<tab>mother<tab>母ちゃんも同じ事いってたな。だからどうしたってんだよ。オレにゃ関係ない。 - <reading> - Mum said the same thing. But, so what? It's got nothing to do with me.

Where to export

  • Any list of entries: long-click an entry to start selecting, select the entries (there is a button that selects them all), then Export as Anki-compatible CSV and pick ?: Custom.
  • The Notepad: Export Notepad as CSV, then Custom, exports the whole Notepad. To export it category by category, select a category's items as in any list.
  • The quiz screen: Export as Anki-compatible CSV exports every entry the quiz settings select, not just one quiz's worth.
  • Aedict Online: the Save As CSV button of a list of entries, an entry, the Notepad or the quiz, then Custom and Download. It needs you to be logged in.

The Custom option shows up only once the format is set. In Aedict, Send to apps hands the file to another app, such as Gmail or Dropbox, and Save to disk saves it as a file.

The built-in formats

The other options are written in the same variables, a good start for your own:

  • 2: Kanji, Reading+English: "%kanji%","%reading%: %sense%"
  • 2: Kanji+Reading, English: "%furigana_anki%","%sense%"
  • 2: English, Kanji+Reading: "%sense%","%furigana_anki%"
  • 3: Kanji, Reading, English: "%kanji%","%reading%","%sense%"
  • 3: English, Kanji, Reading: "%sense%","%kanji%","%reading%"
  • The Notepad's Category;Kanji;Reading;Meaning: "%notepad_category%";"%kanji%","%reading%","%sense%"

With Settings / Export / Export Ruby Furigana checked, the built-in formats write %furigana_anki% where they say %reading%. A custom format writes exactly what it says.

How a format is read

  • Every %variable% from the list below is replaced by the entry's value. Anything else is copied as it is, a misspelled variable such as %kanij% included.
  • \t is replaced by the TAB character. No other escape is: \n stays a backslash and an n, and every entry is one line.
  • Aedict neither quotes nor escapes the values. Meanings are full of commas, so put them in quotes in a comma-separated format, or separate the columns with \t.
  • Readings follow Aedict's romanization: while Aedict shows romaji, the readings come out in romaji.
  • Meanings come in the dictionary language set in Settings, falling back to English. Example sentences and words come in the example sentences' language, their parts separated by Settings / Export / Custom Sentence Separator, " - " unless you change it.

Variables

  • \t - replaced by the TAB character
  • %kanji% - replaced by the japanese form, may contain kanji, hiragana or katakana. May equal to %reading% for hiragana/katakana-only entries. Never empty. If multiple kanjis are present, they are comma-separated. For example 日の出
  • %reading% - entry reading in hiragana or katakana, never empty. If multiple readings are present, they are comma-separated. Example: ひので. For a kanji, its on and kun readings, then its name readings in brackets.
  • %sense% - English (or other lang) translation, for example "(P, n) sunrise". The meanings within one sense are separated by commas, the senses by semicolons.
  • %sense_no_flags% (since Aedict 3.38.5) - English (or other lang) translation with no flags, for example "sunrise"
  • %examples% - At most three example sentences containing %kanji%, separated by a configurable string, in the form of KANJI1 - READING1 - SENSE1 - KANJI2 - READING2 - ... (if the separator is - ). Note that this item greatly slows the export process.
  • %examples_en% (since Aedict 3.50.17) - the translations of the same three example sentences only, in the form of SENSE1 - SENSE2 - SENSE3. Also slows the export down.
  • %examples_jp% (since Aedict 3.50.17) - the Japanese of the same three example sentences only, in the form of KANJI1 - READING1 - KANJI2 - READING2 - ... Also slows the export down.
  • %words% (since Aedict 3.49) - At most three words from JMDict written with the kanji, or with the word's kanji, separated by a configurable string, in the form of KANJI1 - READING1 - SENSE1 - KANJI2 - READING2 - ... (if the separator is - ). Note that this item greatly slows the export process.
  • %strokes% - Stroke count number, 1..30. Empty if the entry does not originate from KanjiDic.
  • %skip% - The skip code, e.g. 4-1-4. Empty if the entry does not originate from KanjiDic.
  • %radical% - The kanji radical character. Empty if the entry does not originate from KanjiDic.
  • %grade% - The kanji grade level. 1 through 6 indicates a Kyouiku kanji and the grade in which the kanji is taught in Japanese schools. 8 indicates it is one of the remaining Jouyou Kanji to be learned in junior high school, and 9 or 10 indicates it is a Jinmeiyou (for use in names) kanji. Empty if the entry does not originate from KanjiDic.
  • %parts% - The kanji parts. Empty if the entry does not originate from KanjiDic.
  • %grammar_codes% (since Aedict 3.4.9): the grammar code string, such as "(P) common, (n) noun (common) (futsuumeishi)"
  • %grammar_codes_short% (since Aedict 3.38.5): the grammar code string, short version, such as "(P, n)"
  • %furigana% (since Aedict 3.4.9): the ruby furigana, in the form of kana{kanji;reading}kana{kanji;reading}kana etc. If there are multiple kanjis with multiple readings, this is a comma-separated list of ruby furigana. If the entry has no kanji, this is just the reading. For example: 勉強を怠ける will be represented in furigana as {勉強;べんきょう}を{怠;なま}ける
  • %notepad_category% (since Aedict 3.4.32): if the entry is stored in the notepad, this will be replaced by the category name of the first category this entry appears in. If not, this will be an empty string.
  • %tag% (since Aedict 3.4.34): replaced by the text the entry is tagged with. If the entry has no tag, this will be an empty string.
  • %tagcolor% (since Aedict 3.39.11): replaced by the color name the entry is tagged with, one of blue, green, magenta, orange, red; any other color as # and its hex code. If the entry has no tag, this will be an empty string.
  • %furigana_anki% (since Aedict 3.19): the ruby furigana in Anki's own notation, kanji[reading], which Anki's {% raw %}{{furigana:Field}}{% endraw %} shows as furigana; a space goes before each kanji that doesn't start the text or follow a bracket. For example, 勉強を怠ける is represented as 勉強[べんきょう]を 怠[なま]ける. If there are multiple kanjis with multiple readings, this is a comma-separated list of ruby furigana. If the entry has no kanji, this is just the reading.
  • %position_in_sentence% (since Aedict 3.19): if the sentence analysis kicked in and produced this entry, this will be the original position in sentence, in the form of start_index,length. E.g. analyzing いろいろなのりものがはったつしました will also produce the word する as the last word, with the position of 14,4. If this entry has not been produced by the sentence analysis, then this will be empty.
  • %deinflection_info% (since Aedict 3.26): the deinflections used to deinflect this word, each one starting with "; ". For example "; あった -> あう, ある (Base Ta: I did X.)". Empty if the word was not deinflected.

The same variables, without %examples% and %words%, come back from Aedict's search when an app asks for the results: see return_results in the API.

Export to AnkiDroid

With AnkiDroid installed, Export to AnkiDroid adds the entries straight into an AnkiDroid deck, no file involved. One entry opens an editor first, where you pick the deck and adjust the furigana, the meaning, the example sentences, the tags and a note of your own. Several entries go into the deck set in Settings / Export / Always Export to Deck, or into one you pick.

On first use Aedict creates its own note type in AnkiDroid, sk.baka.aedict3v8, with three cards per note: Kanji > Kana+English, Kanji+Kana > English and English > Japanese; suspend the ones you don't want in AnkiDroid. Every note is tagged Aedict. The note's fields are filled from the variables above:

  • Expression: %kanji%
  • Reading: %reading%, with the pitch accent marked in HTML when Settings / Export / Export Pitch as HTML is checked
  • Meaning: %sense_no_flags%
  • Furigana: %furigana_anki%
  • Grammar: %grammar_codes%
  • GrammarShort: %grammar_codes_short%
  • Sentence: %examples%
  • AedictTags: %tag%
  • CustomNotes: the note you typed in the editor; empty when exporting several entries
  • Words: %words%

Unless Settings / Export / Allow duplicates is checked, an entry whose Expression is already in a note of this type, in any deck, is skipped.

The cards ask for the Noto Sans JP font: copy it into AnkiDroid's collection.media folder as _NotoSansJP-Regular.otf and _NotoSansJP-Bold.otf. Without them AnkiDroid shows its default font.

Share

Share (First Entry Only) sends one entry to any app, its card front as the subject and its back as the text. Settings / Export / Export Flashcard front/back picks which is which: Kanji, Reading+English (the default), Kanji+Reading, English, English, Kanji+Reading or Kanji, English. The Send as Flashcard swipe action exports the swiped entry to AnkiDroid when it is installed, and shares it otherwise.
